How I Use My Tools

Every piece of software I use has earned its place through real project work. Here is the story behind each one — where I learned it, and how it helped deliver results.

Petrel — Subsurface Modelling & Well Planning

Used at HNA Myanmar Oil & Gas · MPRL E&P training

Petrel is where the subsurface picture comes together. During my time with MPRL E&P and later at HNA, I used Petrel to build and review geological models, visualise structure and reservoir geometry, and support well location proposals.

When we needed to evaluate candidate wells for workover or propose new drilling locations, Petrel gave me the integrated view — combining well data, structure maps, and stratigraphy — that turned raw numbers into a defensible recommendation for management and investors.

SMT Kingdom — Seismic & Well Log Interpretation

Used at GoldPetrol JOC / Interra Resources & MPRL E&P

Kingdom (now an IHS Markit product) was my daily workspace for interpreting well logs and tying them to seismic data. At GoldPetrol JOC, I used it to review well data across the Yenangyaung and Chauk fields — mature, heavily-drilled areas where understanding each well's history is essential.

The software helped me correlate formations, identify zones of interest, and support the weekly operation reports I prepared for the team. It is also where I learned to integrate mud log data with log curves — a skill I carried into every later role.

CLiq — CPT Interpretation & Liquefaction Assessment

Used at MBCCD · Amarapura River City

At MBCCD, CPT was the foundation of our ground investigation work for the Amarapura River City development. CLiq (from GeoLogismiki) is the industry-standard tool for interpreting cone penetration test data, and I used it to process CPT profiles, evaluate soil behaviour type, and assess liquefaction potential.

The results fed directly into ground improvement design — vibro-compaction decisions depended on the soil layers and risk zones CLiq helped me identify. Producing clear CPT reports with CLiq meant the engineering team could act on the data with confidence.

CPeT-IT — Advanced CPT Data Processing

Used at MBCCD · Geotechnical Investigation

CPeT-IT was my companion tool to CLiq. While CLiq focused on liquefaction, CPeT-IT gave me deeper geotechnical parameters — soil unit weight, friction angle, undrained shear strength — derived from CPT data.

Together with CLiq, it let me build a complete picture of the site: what the soil was, how it would behave, and what ground improvement was needed. This dual-tool workflow became my standard approach for every CPT investigation at MBCCD.

AutoCAD — Geological Maps, Well Diagrams & Site Plans

Used at GoldPetrol JOC, Myanma Precious Resources & MBCCD

AutoCAD is the drafting backbone of geological and engineering work. I completed an intermediate AutoCAD course and have used it across multiple roles — drawing well mechanical diagrams at GoldPetrol JOC, preparing geological maps and cross-sections during gold exploration, and supporting geotechnical site plans at MBCCD.

Well diagrams, in particular, are only useful if they are accurate and clearly presented. AutoCAD let me maintain those records precisely, so the operations team always had reliable drawings for every well.

QGIS — Spatial Mapping for Exploration & Fieldwork

Used at Myanma Precious Resources (Modi-Momi) & MAPG

QGIS was central to my exploration work. During gold exploration at Modi-Momi, I used it to overlay geological data, structural trends, and sampling locations — building spatial layers that helped define where to focus drilling.

At MAPG, QGIS supported geological mapping activities, letting the team visualise field data and produce maps for reports and presentations. It is the tool that turns scattered field observations into a coherent exploration story.

Excel & Microsoft Office — Data Analysis & Reporting

Used across all roles

Excel is where most of the raw data lived. At GoldPetrol JOC, I used it to monitor injection and production groups, track well status, and compile the weekly operation reports. At MBCCD, it supported CPT and SPT data tracking.

Word and PowerPoint were equally important — preparing technical reports, summaries, and presentations for senior geologists, department heads, and investors. Clear communication of technical data is a skill I have deliberately built alongside the analysis itself.

🎓 M.Sc. Thesis Research · University of Yangon

Facies Analysis & Reservoir Characterisation of the Miocene Rock Units

Sinlangyi Area, Minbu District, Magway Region, Myanmar
Study Area

Sinlangyi, Minbu District

Basin

Salin Basin (fore-arc sub-basin)

Target Units

Pyawbwe & Kyaukkok Formations

Structural Setting

Htaukshabin Anticline (First Line of Structure)

Methods

Lithofacies, petrography, GR/SP/resistivity logs, seismic

Reservoir Focus

Shale volume, porosity, permeability, water saturation

What the Research Investigated

My M.Sc. thesis examined the facies architecture and reservoir characteristics of the Miocene rock units exposed on the east flank of the Salin Basin, within the middle portion of the Htaukshabin Anticline. The stratigraphic column in the study area spans the Padaung Formation (Middle Oligocene), Okhmintaung Formation (Upper Oligocene), Pyawbwe Formation (Lower Miocene), Kyaukkok Formation (Middle Miocene), and the Irrawaddy Formation (Pliocene–Pleistocene).

The work integrated outcrop lithofacies analysis, petrography (thin-section study of sandstones and clay mineral identification), electrofacies analysis from GR/SP/ resistivity logs of well H-595, and reinterpretation of 2D seismic lines (KNP-7, MH-1, MH-2, HS-1). The structural style was resolved as a positive flower-shape inversion anticline controlled by the East Hading Reverse Fault.

Key Contributions

  • Identified and interpreted multiple lithofacies across the Pyawbwe, Kyaukkok and Irrawaddy Formations — from prodelta/shelf shales to distributary channel and tidal flat sandstones.
  • Used GR log motifs (after Cant, 1992 and Rider, 1999) to recognise progradation, retrogradation, aggradation and petrograding/retrograding patterns in the proven reservoirs.
  • Calculated shale volume (Vsh) using the Larionov (1969) formula, and porosity, permeability and water saturation from resistivity logs using Archie-based relations.
  • Volumetrically estimated Initial Oil In Place (IOIP) and Initial Gas In Place (IGIP) for the Kyaukkok and Padaung reservoirs.
  • Suggested the footwall portion of the East Hading Reverse Fault as a favourable, untested hydrocarbon trap — recommending appraisal drilling.
